By Request

A scenario in which a US central bank digital currency arrives not because it's imposed, but because frightened citizens ask for it.

Statement of record

This page records a scenario I've heard argued and consider possible. It isn't my original idea, and it isn't a claim that this will happen. I'm publishing it with a date so there's a public record of what I believed and when.

If it plays out, this page shows it was foreseeable. If it doesn't, this page shows what I got wrong. Either way, the record stays as written. Later changes will be added as dated updates at the bottom, never edits to the original text.

Fact marks things verifiable today. Scenario marks projection.

The short version

Five steps

1
The planners' frustration

Fact Central banks inject money hoping it gets spent, but much of it gets saved, used to pay down debt, or parked in assets. Their tools for forcing money to circulate are blunt: near-zero or negative interest rates, targeted lending programs, and simply creating more money than the math calls for. Japan has tried this for decades with limited success.

Scenario Programmable digital money is the tool a central planner would design if starting from scratch. It could carry an expiration date, be limited to certain kinds of spending, or have a negative rate applied directly to every balance. It would remove the leak instead of trying to outrun it.

2
The wall: Americans don't want it

Fact In January 2025, a presidential executive order barred federal agencies from establishing or promoting a CBDC. The House passed the Anti-CBDC Surveillance State Act on July 17, 2025, by a 219–210 vote. As of early 2026, the Senate version had not passed. The Federal Reserve has repeatedly said it would not issue a CBDC without authorization from Congress.

Scenario Forcing a CBDC on the public fails politically. The only way through is for the public to demand it.

3
The crisis: an AI-driven banking failure

Fact Bank runs now move at internet speed. In March 2023, depositors tried to pull about $42 billion from Silicon Valley Bank in a single day, coordinated largely through phones and social media, and the bank failed the next morning. AI is already used for voice-cloning fraud, deepfake identity theft, and automated cyberattacks.

Scenario A banking crisis hits that's driven by AI, or blamed on it: mass account takeovers, AI-coordinated runs, attacks that drain accounts faster than any bank can respond. Whether the crisis is deliberately engineered or simply exploited once it happens, the result is the same. Ordinary people lose faith that private banks can keep their money safe.

4
The request

Scenario Frightened citizens and their representatives call for a safe, government-backed digital dollar: identity-verified, fraud-proof, reversible, and guaranteed by the government itself. The CBDC arrives as consumer protection, and the same laws that blocked it get repealed or worked around with public approval.

5
The price

Scenario Every feature that made it "safe" is also a control. Identity verification means every transaction is visible. Reversibility means any payment can be frozen. Programmability means money can be set to expire, restricted to approved purchases, or charged a negative rate directly. The tool the planners couldn't impose, the public asked for.

Groundwork

Pieces already in place

None of these proves the scenario. They're the real, current developments it would build on.

  • Fact The GENIUS Act (July 2025) bans payment stablecoin issuers from paying holders interest. Bank-issued deposit tokens and tokenized Treasuries can still pay yield, which puts the yield-paying digital dollars on bank-controlled rails.
  • Fact The Treasury has shifted its borrowing toward short-term bills, the part of the debt market most tightly tied to Fed policy.
  • Fact Other countries already run CBDCs or large pilots. China's digital yuan (e-CNY) is the largest.
  • Scenario Read together, these steer digital dollars toward rails the government and banks control. That makes a final step to a CBDC smaller than it looks today.
Why this could be wrong

The best case against it

  • Opposition crosses party lines. Distrust of government financial surveillance comes from both the left and the right, and repealing a CBDC ban would be a hard vote even in a crisis.
  • Banks don't want it either. A government digital dollar would let people hold money directly with the central bank, pulling deposits away from commercial banks. Banks have every reason to fight it and push their own deposit tokens instead.
  • A crisis could push the other way. A collapse in trust in banks could send people toward self-custody — Bitcoin, gold, cash — instead of toward more central control.
  • "Engineered" is the weakest link. A crisis that's exploited after the fact is plausible. One that's deliberately planned requires coordination that's hard to keep secret. The scenario still works without the engineering, but that part is a belief, not evidence.
What would confirm it

Signs this is unfolding

  • A major banking incident publicly blamed on AI: account takeovers, an AI-coordinated run, or a large-scale automated attack.
  • Polling or prominent public calls for a "government-backed" or "safe" digital dollar after such an incident.
  • The January 2025 executive order reversed, or the anti-CBDC bill quietly dropped.
  • The Fed resuming CBDC research or announcing a pilot.
  • CBDC-style legislation framed as consumer protection or fraud prevention rather than as monetary policy.
  • Identity-linked digital wallets becoming required for government payments such as tax refunds or benefits.
